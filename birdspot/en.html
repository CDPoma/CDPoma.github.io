What BirdSpot is

BirdSpot is a birdwatching app for iPhone, iPad and Macs with Apple silicon. It tells you which birds can be seen in your area today, depending on the time of year, identifies the bird in a photo with AI, and shows bird hides and Natura 2000 bird protection areas on an Apple satellite map. Keep your sightings in a journal with your life list and share them with friends. It never publishes where birds nest or where the most threatened species live.

Important: the AI can be wrong, and the protection information the app gives is for Spain. Watch from a distance, with no playback lures or bait, and check the rules of the place you visit: breeding seasons, drones and access change from site to site.

What you get

  • Today's birds, near you: The Today screen lists the birds of your area with their name in your language, their scientific name and an illustration, with this month's birds first: passing through on migration, just arriving, residents or winter visitors. They come from public GBIF records for the cell (about 25 km) where you are, or the area you left on the map. Tap a bird for its page: the months it is seen, photos people have shared and how to watch it without disturbing it.
  • Which bird is it? Take a photo: Snap a photo or pick one from your library: the AI gives the common and scientific name with a confidence percentage, and warns you when it is not sure. It adds the features visible in the photo, the look-alikes and how to tell them apart, its protection status in Spain from the Spanish Catalogue of Threatened Species, and an ethical-watching tip. If the photo shows a nest or a chick, it asks you to keep away and saves the place for your eyes only.
  • A birding map on 3D satellite imagery: On top of Apple's 3D satellite imagery you can switch layers on and off: Bird hides and observation towers (almost 8,000 from OpenStreetMap, in 26 European countries), Natura 2000 Special Protection Areas (European Environment Agency) with a breeding-season and drone reminder, and Birds of the area: an illustration of the bird easiest to see in each area this month, with a "+" that opens the photos people have shared there and the other birds. Press and hold a point to see its birds, save it as a spot or head there.
  • Your journal and life list: Every sighting is saved with its photo, species, date and place. Your life list gathers the different species you have seen, with the first date for each. Your spots are private; save them with GPS or on the map, and "Go" guides you there with a map and a compass showing bearing and distance.
  • Friends and a feed of sightings: Add friends with a code, with no email or password: just a name. The feed shows everyone's sightings, your friends' or just yours. When you save a sighting you choose who sees where you saw it: only you, your friends or everyone. Anyone can report a sighting or block its author.
  • Safe for birds: Endangered species never appear with their location, and vulnerable ones only with a rough area of about 10 km (about 6 miles). The place of a nest or a chick is never shared, and an ethics reminder covers how to watch: from a distance, with no lures or bait, and no drones where they are restricted.

Free and Pro

Free, with a banner and a short full-screen video before each identification. Every feature is in the free version. BirdSpot Pro, a monthly or yearly subscription, removes the banner and the video; the price in your currency is shown in the App Store before you subscribe, with a 7-day free trial if you have not tried it before. There is a limit of 40 identifications per day per account, with or without Pro.

Platforms

Works on iPhone, iPad and Macs with Apple silicon, in Spanish (Spain and Latin America), English, Brazilian Portuguese, German, French and Italian. It needs an internet connection to load the birds of your area, identify and share. A Mac has no compass: the Go screen gives you bearing and distance.

Your data

The account is just a display name, with no email or password, and the Today screen and the map work without a name. The photo you use to identify is not kept unless you save the sighting. Spots are private until you share them. The photo, species and date of a saved sighting show in the feed, and you choose who sees the place; nests, chicks and endangered species are not published. Your exact position does not leave your phone to show you the birds of your area. Read it all in the privacy policy.

What it does not do

  • It does not identify bird song or any sound: photos only.
  • It does not say where birds nest or where the most threatened species live.
  • The protection it shows is Spain's (Spanish Catalogue of Threatened Species); it does not include other countries'.
  • It does not replace a field guide or an ornithologist: the AI can be wrong, especially with distant or blurry photos.
  • It does not replace the rules of the place you visit: the limits of the protected areas and the breeding season are set by the authorities.
  • Offline it cannot identify or load the birds of a new area; it shows what was last saved.
  • For now it is only on the App Store, for iPhone, iPad and Macs with Apple silicon.

FAQ

Which app tells you which birds you can see today near you?

BirdSpot, for iPhone, iPad and Macs with Apple silicon. Its Today screen lists the birds of your area for the time of year, with their name in your language, and puts this month's birds first: passing through, just arriving, resident or winter visitors. The data are public GBIF records for a cell of about 25 km: where there are few records, the list can be short.

How can I find out which bird it is from a photo?

With BirdSpot's Identify button: take a photo or choose one from your library and the artificial intelligence (Claude, by Anthropic) returns the common and scientific name of the most likely bird with a confidence percentage, and warns you when it is low. It adds the features visible in the photo, the look-alikes and how to tell them apart. It is a guide and can be wrong. It helps if the whole bird is in the frame, with the beak and plumage clearly visible.

Does BirdSpot identify bird songs?

No. This version identifies from photos only. It does not analyse sounds or recordings.

Does BirdSpot publish where nests or threatened birds are?

No. Endangered species never appear with their location, and vulnerable ones only with a rough area of about 10 km. If a photo shows a nest or a chick, the app asks you to keep away and saves the place for you only, and only the author can see those sightings. On a species page, where it has been seen is shown as areas, never exact points.

Where can I find bird hides and protected bird areas on a map?

On BirdSpot's Map tab, over Apple's 3D satellite imagery: the Bird hides layer shows almost 8,000 bird hides and observation towers from OpenStreetMap in 26 European countries, and the SPA and Natura 2000 layer shows the European Union's bird protection areas (European Environment Agency), with a breeding-season and drone reminder. Tap a bird hide to save it as a spot or get there.

Is BirdSpot free?

Yes. The download is free and every feature is in the free version, with a banner and a short video before each identification. BirdSpot Pro removes them, as a monthly or yearly subscription with a 7-day free trial if you have not tried it before; the App Store shows the price in your currency. With or without Pro, there is a limit of 40 identifications per day per account.

Does BirdSpot work outside Spain?

Yes. The birds of your area come from public GBIF records from anywhere, although where there are few records the list is short. The legal protection the app shows is Spain's, the SPAs and Natura 2000 are European Union sites and the bird hides are in 26 European countries.

Do I need an account or an email address?

There is no email and no password. Today and the map work without a name. To identify, save sightings or spots and have friends, the app asks only for a display name. You can delete your account and all its data from the Friends tab, in the Settings gear.
