BirdSpot · Help & support

Summary: BirdSpot tells you which birds you can see in your area today, identifies the bird in a photo with AI, shows bird hides and bird protection areas on a satellite map and lets you keep a journal with your life list and share it with friends. The Today screen and the map work without a name; to identify, save sightings or spots and have friends you need an account, which has no email or password: it is just a display name you choose. You choose who sees each whole sighting (photo, species, note, date and place): only you, your friends or everyone; only what you put in "everyone" reaches the public feed, which can be seen also without an account. Nests, chicks and endangered species are not published: only their author sees them and their place is never shared. Your spots are private until you decide to share them. To identify, the photo is sent to our server and from there to Claude, by Anthropic; we do not keep it unless you save the sighting. The app shows Google AdMob ads and the Pro subscription removes them. Usage statistics are anonymous.

Who is responsible

Gabriel Micó Victoria, developer of BirdSpot. For any question about your data, or to exercise your rights, write to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com.

Account

The Today screen opens an anonymous Supabase session in the background, without a name, so it can ask our server for the birds of your area. To identify, save sightings or spots, add friends, report or block you need to choose a display name (1 to 40 characters) and accept the community guidelines; that same session becomes your account. It has no email or password. Other people see your name on your sightings, on the spots you share and on your friend requests: we recommend a nickname rather than your full name. The session is kept on your device: if you delete the app, you start from scratch. You can delete your account in Friends → ⚙ → Delete my account. Your account and data are stored on Supabase, on a server in the European Union (Frankfurt).

The birds of your area (Today screen)

To show you the birds of your area, BirdSpot uses your position inside the device and sends our server only the centre of a 0.25-degree cell (about 25 km) that contains it, never your exact position. If you do not give location permission, the area where you left the map is used. Our server queries GBIF for the public records of that cell and keeps the result for up to 30 days in a shared cache that holds no account or personal data. The last area loaded is kept on your device so it can be shown when there is no connection. To stop abuse, the server also counts requests per account and per IP address (the latter only as a hashed fingerprint).

Identifying a bird

When you take or choose a photo in "Identify", the app makes a reduced copy of it (about 1,024 pixels on the longer side) and re-encodes it as JPEG, which removes its metadata, such as the position where it was taken. That copy is sent to our server, together with the app language, and from there to Anthropic's artificial intelligence service (Claude), which returns the likely species, its confidence, the visible features and the look-alikes. The protection status in Spain and the ethics tip are added by our server from our own tables. The photo is not saved to your photo library.

  • We do not keep the photo or the result on our server unless you save the sighting.
  • We do not give Anthropic your account, your name or your position.
  • Identifying needs a name (the anonymous session of Today cannot identify). We count how many identifications each account makes per day (the limit is 40 per account per day) and, to stop abuse, there is also a daily cap per IP address, stored only as a hashed fingerprint (HMAC), never the plain IP. Those counters do not include the photo or the result.
  • The identification can be wrong: it is not a guarantee and does not replace a field guide.

Location and compass

BirdSpot asks for location permission while you use the app, not in the background: to know which birds are in your area, put you on the map, guide you with the "Go" screen, save a spot with GPS or mark where you saw a bird when you save a sighting. Your exact position reaches our server only when you save a spot with GPS or save a sighting with a location. The compass, bearing and distance are calculated on your device. If you do not give permission, you can mark the place by hand on the map; you can also save a sighting without a location, if you confirm it.

Sightings

When you save a sighting we store the photo (a copy of up to 1,600 pixels), the species, its scientific name, the confidence, the situation in which it was seen (perched, in flight, nest, chick, injured or other), your optional note and the date. You choose who sees the whole sighting: only me (by default), my friends or everyone. With "only me", nobody else sees the photo, species, note, date or place. With "my friends", only your friends. With "everyone" it shows in the public feed, with your display name, also to people without an account.

Photos are stored in a public storage area of our server. The address of a photo is not shown to anyone who cannot see the sighting, but whoever already has the link to a photo can open it for as long as it exists; it is deleted when you delete the sighting or your account. In addition, our server applies these rules to protect birds, whatever the app says:

  • Nests, chicks and endangered species (according to the Spanish Catalogue of Threatened Species): only the author sees the sighting, it does not appear in the feed or in the photos of a map area, and its place is saved for you only.
  • Vulnerable species: if you share the place, it is stored blurred to about 10 km (0.1 degrees) and the exact coordinates are not kept.
  • If the server cannot tell which species it is, the place is saved for you only.
  • On species pages and in the sheet for a map area, where a bird has been seen is shown as areas, never as exact points or coordinates.

Your spots

A spot stores its name, an optional note and its coordinates, and whether you marked it with GPS or by hand, tied to your account on our server. The bird hides on the map and any point can also be saved as a spot. A spot is private: only you see it, until you decide otherwise:

  • With a friend: the friends you choose see it, with your display name. You can stop sharing it.
  • On your wall: anyone sees it, also without an account. You can take it down whenever you like.
  • By WhatsApp or another app: BirdSpot opens the system share sheet with the name and coordinates. What happens next depends on the app you choose.
  • A spot saved from a nest or an endangered species cannot be made public or shared.

You can delete a spot from its page. Deleting your account deletes all your spots.

Friends

Each account has a 6-character friend code. Anyone with your code can send you a friend request, and you are friends only if you accept it. Your friends see your display name, the spots you share with them and the sightings you mark "My friends". You can remove a friend: what had been shared between the two of you stops being shared.

The map and third-party services

On iPhone, iPad and Mac the map is Apple's (MapKit), with its 3D satellite imagery. Apple receives the map requests, the area you are looking at and your IP address. We give it no account or identifier of ours, and BirdSpot does not send it your spots or sightings: the pins and illustrations are placed by the app on top of the map on your device.

On top of the map there are layers you switch on or off. "Bird hides" comes from OpenStreetMap (ODbL licence) and that data is built into the app: it is not requested from the internet. "SPA" is drawn with image tiles served by the European Environment Agency: that server receives your IP address and the part of the map you are looking at (the tiles requested), like any web server; never your account or your name, and only while the layer is on.

Bird records are queried by our server, not by your phone: GBIF (public records under CC0 and CC BY licences) to know which birds are in a cell and when, and common names from Wikidata (CC0), which we load into our database beforehand. Neither GBIF nor Wikidata receives your account or your position.

Checking for a new version

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, when the app starts it asks Apple's App Store (itunes.apple.com) which is the latest version and, if there is a version with important changes, tells you and takes you to its page. Apple receives the app identifier and your IP address. If the check fails, the app keeps working.

Reporting and blocking

You can report any public sighting or spot (for dangerous information, offensive content, spam or another reason) and block its author. Blocking someone hides their sightings from you in the feed. Whatever collects 3 reports is hidden automatically and kept for the developer to review, within 24 hours.

Deleting your data

  • One sighting: delete it from its page. It is removed together with its photo and its location.
  • One spot: delete it from its page.
  • Your whole account: Friends → ⚙ → Delete my account. Your name, sightings with their locations and photos, spots and what you shared of them, friendships and requests, reports and blocks and your usage counter are deleted.

Ads and purchases

The app shows Google AdMob ads in the free version: a banner at the bottom and a full-screen ad while each photo is being identified. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent before showing ads; then iOS asks whether you allow tracking (App Tracking Transparency). You can change your answer whenever you like, and the "Ad privacy options" item in Settings lets you reopen the form. If you do not allow tracking, you still see ads, but not personalised ones. Google may receive data from your device and the ads according to its privacy policy.

Pro is a subscription managed by Apple's App Store, with RevenueCat to verify your purchase: RevenueCat receives the anonymous identifier of your account and your purchases, not your name or your location. With Pro no ads are shown.

Usage statistics

We use Firebase Analytics, anonymously, to see which features are used and improve the app: for example, that an identification was made, that a sighting was saved or deleted, that an ad was seen or that Pro was bought. Your name, your coordinates, the names of the birds and the text of your notes are never sent. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland they are switched on only if you allow it in Google's form, and they are never used for advertising.

What is kept on your device

Your account session, your settings (language, map layers, map view, last tab), the last bird area loaded and whether you have Pro are kept on the device itself, so the app comes back the way you left it. The bird hides (from OpenStreetMap) and the bird illustrations are built into the app.

Your rights

You can ask for access, correction or deletion of your data by writing to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com, although you can already do almost everything from the app itself.

Notice: Important: the AI can be wrong, and the protection information the app gives is for Spain. Watch from a distance, with no playback lures or bait, and check the rules of the place you visit: breeding seasons, drones and access change from site to site.
