What GoldSpot is

GoldSpot is an app for iPhone and iPad that shows on a world map where placer gold (the kind found in streams and rivers) is most likely to be, together with known gold mines. Each spot has a score from 0 to 100 and the factors that explain it. It is an indicative estimate: it does not guarantee that there is gold.

Important: GoldSpot's score is an indicative, relative estimate, not a probability or a guarantee that there is gold. Prospecting for gold is regulated: get any permits you need and respect the law, private property and protected areas. Abandoned mines are dangerous: do not enter. A photo is not enough to tell whether something is gold: confirm it with home tests or an assay.

What you get

  • Spots with an explained score: More than 46,000 zones around the world with a score from 0 to 100 and the factors behind it: source rock upstream, distance to a stream, old terraces, nearby mines and more. The score is relative: it compares places with each other and is not a probability.
  • Known gold mines: More than 95,000 gold mines, workings and occurrences from public geological surveys, with their status (active, abandoned or unknown) and their source. Spots and mines are bundled in the app and can be browsed offline.
  • Analyse this point: Press and hold anywhere on the map, or use “My location”, and GoldSpot works out on your phone, for free and without ads, the score of that point using the terrain around it. Without a connection it gives a partial score with low confidence.
  • Identify a mineral from a photo: Take a photo of a stone, a nugget or what is left in your pan and the AI tells you what mineral it looks like, whether it could be gold (as opposed to pyrite, chalcopyrite or golden mica) and how to check it at home. It never promises gold: when in doubt, it says “doubtful”. Up to 40 analyses per day per account.
  • A map to read the terrain: Apple's satellite imagery in 3D, with layers you switch on or off: towns and roads, trails and tracks, streams and springs and contour lines, plus the heat map, the geology and the stream network. With the crosshair you read the elevation, the slope and the aspect of a point.
  • Your places and your finds, also anonymous: Save your places (private until you share them), get to them with a map and a compass, and post spots and nuggets with up to 8 photos, without your name if you like: with the time rounded to the day and the photos stripped of metadata. Add friends with a code.

Free

Free to download. Before each AI analysis of a mineral you watch a short full-screen video (a Google AdMob rewarded ad, on iPhone and iPad); without it, the photo is not analysed. Analysing a point on the map and the rest of the app have no ads. For now there are no purchases or subscriptions. Up to 40 photo analyses per day per account.

Platforms

Works on iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 15 or later. An internet connection is needed for the map and satellite imagery, the terrain used by “Analyse this point”, identifying minerals and posting; spots and mines can be browsed offline.

Your data

Your account is just a name, with no email or password. Your spots are private until you share them. What you post as anonymous carries neither your name nor the exact time and its photos are stripped of metadata, although the developer can see who posted it. The photo you identify a mineral with goes to Anthropic (Claude) and is not kept unless you publish it. Your location only leaves your phone when you save a spot or post with a location. The privacy policy explains everything.

What it does not do

  • It does not guarantee that there is gold: the score is an indicative, relative estimate, and a photo is not enough to tell whether something is gold.
  • It does not give permission to prospect anywhere and does not check protected areas for you: respect the law and the permits of each place.
  • It does not identify a mineral without a connection or without first watching the video.
  • It does not publish your spots: they are private until you share them with friends or on the wall.
  • It does not hide who posts from the developer: anonymity holds against other users, not against the server operator.

Frequently asked questions

Does GoldSpot guarantee that I will find gold?

No. The score is an indicative, relative estimate that compares places with each other; it is not a probability. A zone shows where to look, not where to dig, and a nearby mine may mean nothing if it is exhausted. A photo is not enough to tell whether something is gold either: the AI identification is a guide and must be confirmed with home tests or an assay.

What is a spot and how is the score worked out?

A spot is a zone on the map (a point with a radius) where, according to the calculation, placer gold is more likely. The score, from 0 to 100, adds up factors such as the source rock upstream, the distance to a stream, the size of the catchment, valley floors, old terraces, breaks of slope, confluences, alluvial deposits, nearby gold mines and gold in sediments, and each spot's page says why it appears. A spot can be scientific (the calculation says so), prospectors' (clues such as old workings or official panning areas) or both.

How does GoldSpot identify a mineral?

From a photo you take with the camera or pick from your library. A function on our server sends it to an artificial intelligence service (Claude, by Anthropic) that says what mineral it looks like, and, if Claude does not respond, to Google Gemini as a fallback; what signs are visible, whether it could be gold and which safe home tests can confirm it. It always considers lookalikes such as pyrite, chalcopyrite and golden mica, and when in doubt it answers “doubtful”. There is a limit of 40 analyses per day per account, and an internet connection is required.

Can I post spots and finds without anyone knowing they are mine?

As far as other users are concerned, yes: “Post anonymously” is on by default. Anonymous posts carry no author, show the day and not the time, and their photos are stripped of metadata on the server. There are limits: the developer, who runs the server, can see which account posted each item, and the content of a photo (a face, a recognisable landscape) cannot be cleaned. The privacy policy explains it in detail.

Can I prospect for gold anywhere?

No. Prospecting for gold is regulated: in many places you need a permit or a licence, the land may be privately owned or under a mining claim, and in protected areas or some watercourses it may be forbidden. GoldSpot does not give permission for anything and does not check protected areas for you: find out before you go and respect the law and the permits of each place.

Is it free, and which devices does it run on?

It is free to download and runs on iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 15 or later. Before each AI analysis of a mineral you watch a short Google AdMob video; there are no purchases or subscriptions for now. Analysing a point on the map is free and has no ads.
