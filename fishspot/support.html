FishSpot · Privacy policy

Safety first: FishSpot is a helper, not a regulation. The AI can be wrong and the tide is a prediction without wind or waves: do not rely on the app alone to go out to sea or fish from rocks, and always check your local fishing rules (sizes, closed seasons, bag limits and licence).

FishSpot puts tides, currents, waves and wind, more than 9,700 fishing places and the fish recorded in each area on one 3D satellite map, and with a photo the AI tells you which fish you caught. You save your spots and publish your catches, choosing who sees the place. Something not working, or have an idea? Write to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com.

Frequently asked questions

How do I identify a fish?

On the Identify tab, tap "Take photo" or "Choose from library". Photograph the whole fish, from the side and in good light; if you can, show the head and fins too. The app gives you the probable species, with its confidence, what it can be confused with, whether it can be eaten, whether it is dangerous to touch because of spines or venom, and whether it is invasive or protected. It can be wrong: always double-check. There is a limit of 40 identifications per day per account, and an internet connection and a name on your profile are required.

What is the video I see before an analysis?

The free version shows a short full-screen video before each photo analysis, and a banner at the bottom. With FishSpot Pro both go away. If no video is available (for example without a connection), the photo is not analysed: you can try again or go Pro.

Do I have to create an account?

To identify, save spots, publish, add friends, report or block, yes. The account does not ask for an email or a password: it is anonymous and only has a display name you choose. If you delete the app, you start from scratch. You can see the map, the tides and the public catches without an account.

What layers does the map have?

The background is Apple's satellite imagery, in 3D. On top you switch eight layers on or off from the map options button: Towns and roads, Trails and tracks, Rivers and lakes, Tides, Sea direction, Fish in the area, Fishing places and Contour lines. With contour lines on, point the crosshair and it gives you the elevation, the slope and the aspect of the point. The data is approximate: do not use it as your only guide.

How do the tides work?

They are calculated on your device for more than 3,600 stations in 110 countries: high, mid or low tide, whether it is rising or falling, its height and the next high and low water. Tap a station to see the 24-hour curve. It is an astronomical prediction, without wind or pressure. Where the tide is only centimetres, as in the Mediterranean, the app tells you so.

What is "Sea direction"?

Animated particles that follow the current, the waves or the wind. At each point you see the current in knots and its direction, the wave in metres with its period and the wind in km/h. The data comes from HYCOM and NOAA, is updated on our server several times a day, and each reading carries its time. It is a model: it does not replace the marine forecast.

What happens if I touch and hold a point on the map?

The card of that point opens with its tide, the sea and the fish in the area, and the buttons "Save as my spot" and "Go". The app also reopens on the last tab you used.

Where do the fishing places and the fish of each area come from?

The fishing places were mapped by people on OpenStreetMap: they are not other users' spots and they do not guarantee access or permission, so check access and the rules before going. The fish of the area are species recorded nearby according to GBIF, with an indicative season when known: they are records, not a guarantee that there are fish now.

Does FishSpot tell me the minimum sizes, closed seasons or whether I need a licence?

No. They change by area and year. The app reminds you to check the rules for your area and links to the BOE (marine fishing, Spain) and MITECO (invasive species). The only similar thing is the closed-season notice for salmon when it is running upriver and there are records nearby, and that does not replace the local rules either.

How do I save a spot?

On the Map, use "Save here" to save where you are with GPS, or touch and hold a point (or a fishing place) and tap "Save as my spot". You can also go to Profile → My spots → "Add on the map", or tick "Save this place to My spots" when you publish a catch. Give it a name and, if you like, a note.

Are my spots private? How do I share them?

Yes: a spot is yours alone until you decide to share it. On its page, "Share" lets you choose between "With a friend", "On my wall" (anyone can see it) and "By WhatsApp or another app". You can stop sharing it whenever you like.

How do I publish a catch and who sees the place?

Identify the fish and tap "Publish on the wall". The photo, the description and the species are public. You see the place by default: you can choose "My friends" or "Everyone" before publishing, and it cannot be changed afterwards (delete the catch and publish it again). You can tick "I released it" and "Save this place to My spots".

How do I add friends?

On the Profile tab you have your 6-character friend code. Give it to anyone you like: they type it into "Add by code" and you get the request. You are friends once you accept it.

Why does it ask for my location?

Only while you use the app: to put you on the map and in the "Near you" list, to save a spot with GPS, to publish a catch with its location and for the "Go" screen. If you do not grant permission, you can mark the place by hand on the map or publish without a location, after confirming.

How do I get to a spot?

Open the spot, the catch or the fishing place and tap "Go": you will see the map with a line to the destination and, below it, the compass with the bearing and the distance. Tap "Turn on compass" if needed. "Directions" opens your maps app.

Does it work on a Mac?

Yes, on Macs with Apple silicon, as an iPhone and iPad app. The map, tides, spots and catches work the same. A Mac has no compass, so the "Go" screen gives you the bearing and the distance, and if your Mac has no camera you can identify from a photo in your library.

Does it work without internet?

Not fully. A connection is needed to identify, load the map and the sea, and share. The tide stations and the fishing places are built into the app.

How do I delete a catch or a spot?

Open its page and delete it. A catch is removed together with its photo and its location.

How do I delete my account?

In Profile → ⚙ Settings → Delete my account. Your profile, catches with their photos and locations, spots, friendships, reports and blocks are deleted. It cannot be undone.

How do I remove the ads?

With the FishSpot Pro subscription, from Settings. The banner and the videos go away as soon as the purchase is active. The price is shown in the app before you pay, with a 7-day free trial when the App Store offers it.

I bought Pro and it does not show up

Go to Settings → FishSpot Pro → Restore purchases. Sometimes the store takes a few minutes to confirm the payment. If it still does not appear, write to us with the Apple receipt.

How do I cancel the Pro subscription?

It is managed from your Apple account: iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions → FishSpot Pro.

What if a catch or a spot is inappropriate?

Tap "Report" on its page. When it collects 3 reports it is hidden automatically and the developer reviews it. You can also block its author so you do not see their posts.

Where the data comes from

  • Map and satellite imagery: Apple Maps (MapKit).
  • Rivers, lakes, trails and tracks: © OpenStreetMap contributors, served by OpenFreeMap (with OpenMapTiles). The fishing places are also from OpenStreetMap (ODbL licence).
  • Terrain and contour lines: NASA SRTM and other open sources, via AWS Terrain Tiles.
  • Tides: harmonic constants from the Neaps tide database (with data from Kartverket and TICON-4, CC BY 4.0 licence), calculated on your device.
  • Current, waves and wind: HYCOM, NOAA (WaveWatch III and GFS) and PacIOOS.
  • Fish in the area: GBIF records (CC0 and CC BY) and common names from Wikidata (CC0).
