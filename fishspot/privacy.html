FishSpot · Help & support

Summary: FishSpot shows you tides, sea conditions and fishing places on a satellite map, lets you save your places (spots) and uses AI to identify the fish you caught from a photo. To identify, save spots or publish you need an account, which has no email or password: it is only a display name you choose. The map and public catches can be viewed without an account. Your spots are private until you decide to share them with your friends or on your wall. For each catch you choose who sees where you caught it (only you, your friends or everyone), but the photo, the description and the probable species of a published catch are public to anyone, even without an account. To identify, the photo is sent to our server and from there to Claude, by Anthropic; we do not keep it unless you publish the catch. The app shows Google AdMob ads, and the Pro subscription removes them. Usage statistics are anonymous.

Who is responsible

Gabriel Micó Victoria, developer of FishSpot. For any question about your data, or to exercise your rights, write to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com.

Account

To identify, save spots, publish, add friends, report or block, you need an account. The account has no email and no password: it is an anonymous Supabase session with only a display name that you choose (1 to 40 characters) and that other people see on your catches, on the spots you share and in your friend requests. We recommend a nickname rather than your full name. The session is stored on your device: if you delete the app, you start from scratch, and you can delete your account in Settings → Delete my account. Your account and your data are stored in Supabase, on a server in the European Union (Frankfurt).

Identifying a fish

When you take or pick a photo in "Identify", the app reduces it (longest side at most 1,600 pixels), re-encodes it as a JPEG, which strips the photo's metadata such as the position where it was taken, and sends it to our server, and from there to Anthropic's artificial intelligence service (Claude), which returns the probable species, its confidence and the warnings. The photo is not saved to your library. It is only used to give you the identification.

  • We do not keep the photo or the result on our server unless you publish the catch.
  • We count how many identifications each account makes per day (the limit is 40 per account per day), to apply the usage limit. That counter does not include the photo or the result.
  • The identification can be wrong: it is not a fishing regulation nor a guarantee that a fish is safe to eat.

Location and compass

FishSpot asks for location permission while you use the app, not in the background: when you open the Map and the Catches tab, to place you on the map and show the distance to what is near you; on the "Go" screen, which uses your position and the compass to lead you to a place; when you save a spot with GPS and when you publish a catch with a location. Your position only reaches our server when you save a spot with "Save here" or publish a catch with a location. The compass, the bearing, the distance and the tide of each point are calculated on your device. If you do not grant permission, you can mark the place by hand on the map; you can also publish without a location, if you confirm. When you touch and hold a point on the map, switch on the "Fish in the area" layer or open the fish of a place, the app sends our server the coordinates of that map point (not your position, unless it is right there) to look up the species recorded nearby; more on that below.

Your spots

A spot stores its name, an optional note, its coordinates and whether you marked it with GPS or by hand on the map, linked to your account on our server. The fishing places on the map, and any point on the map, can also be saved as a spot. A spot is private: only you see it, until you decide otherwise:

  • With a friend: the friends you choose see it, with your display name. You can stop sharing it, and the person who received it can also remove it from their list.
  • On your wall: anyone sees it, even without an account, with your display name. You can take it off the wall whenever you like.
  • By WhatsApp or another app: FishSpot opens the system share menu with the name, the coordinates and an Apple Maps link. What happens next depends on the app you choose and on whoever receives it.

You can delete a spot from its page. When you delete your account, all your spots are deleted.

Friends

Each account has a 6-character friend code that only you see. Anyone with your code can send you a friend request, and you are only friends if you accept it. Your friends see your display name, the spots you share with them and the place of the catches for which you choose "My friends". You can remove a friend or block them: doing so stops what had been shared between the two of you.

Publishing a catch

When you publish, we store the photo, the description (optional), the probable species, its scientific name, the confidence, what the identification said about whether it can be eaten and whether it is dangerous to touch, whether it is invasive or protected, whether it lives in sea or rivers, the optional "I released it" mark and the date. All of this is public: anyone can see it, also without an account, in the public catches, with your display name. The photo is stored in a public storage area of our server: anyone who knows its address can open it.

The location is separate. If you grant permission, the GPS position of the catch is stored and you choose who sees the place: only you (the default), your friends or everyone. The place is stored even if you choose "Only me", so that you can see it; nobody else can. After publishing, that choice cannot be changed: to change it, delete the catch and publish it again. If you tick "Save this place to My spots", the place is also saved as a private spot.

  • Without location permission, the app warns you and you can publish without a location, if you confirm.
  • Do not publish photos of people or data you do not want everyone to see. If you leave the place visible to everyone, it may reveal where you fish.

The map, the tides and the sea

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, the maps use Apple's map (MapKit), with its satellite imagery in 3D. Apple receives the map requests, the area you are looking at and your IP address. We do not give Apple any account or identifier of ours, and FishSpot does not send it your spots or your catches: the app places the pins on the map on your device.

On top of the map there are layers you switch on or off. "Towns and roads" are the names on Apple's own map. "Rivers and lakes" and "Trails and tracks" use OpenStreetMap data served by OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org). "Contour lines" are computed on your device from the elevation tiles of AWS Terrain Tiles (Amazon S3, based on open elevation data). Each of these servers receives your IP address and the area of the map you look at (the tiles requested), like any web server; never your account or your name.

Tides are calculated on your device from the constants of about 3,600 stations that ship inside the app: nothing is requested from the internet to calculate them. The fishing places (from OpenStreetMap, ODbL license) also ship inside the app.

The sea data (current, waves and wind) is downloaded several times a day by a function on our server (Supabase) from HYCOM and NOAA (with PacIOOS) and left as images in public storage; the app downloads those images from our server and works out what happens at each point on your device. Your position is not sent for that.

For "Fish in the area", the app asks our server (Supabase) for the species recorded near the area you are looking at, sending the coordinates of that point. Our server looks them up in GBIF and Wikidata and keeps the result so it does not repeat the lookup. That request carries no name, no spots and no catches.

If you tap "Directions by car", the app opens your maps app with the coordinates of the place; from there, that app's privacy policy applies.

Checking for a new version

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, when the app starts it asks Apple's App Store (itunes.apple.com) whether a newer version exists and, if an update is required, tells you and takes you to the store page. For that, Apple receives the app identifier and your IP address, like any web server.

Reporting and blocking

You can report any public catch or spot and block its author. Blocking hides their posts from you and ends the friendship and anything shared between the two of you. A catch or a spot that collects 3 reports is hidden automatically and kept for review by the developer.

Deleting your data

  • One catch: delete it from its page. It is removed together with its photo and its location.
  • One spot: delete it from its page.
  • Your whole account: Settings → Delete my account. Your profile, catches with their locations, spots and what you shared of them, friendships and requests, reports and blocks, usage counter and all the photos in your folder on our server are deleted. The sign-in account is deleted too, unless you use it in another of our apps. It cannot be undone. The purchase record held by Apple and RevenueCat is not deleted from the app.

Ads and purchases

The app shows Google AdMob ads: a banner at the bottom and a video you watch before each photo is analysed. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent before showing ads; you can change it in the same form. On iPhone and iPad, Apple asks whether you allow tracking (ATT): the advertising identifier is only used if you allow it and, if you do not, ads still appear but are not personalised with that tracking. With Pro there are no ads and neither the form nor the tracking permission is asked for.

Pro is a subscription managed by Apple's App Store, with RevenueCat to verify your purchase: RevenueCat receives the anonymous identifier of your account, not your name. Pro removes the banner and the videos. Apple handles the payment; we only learn whether the subscription is active.

Usage statistics

We use Firebase Analytics anonymously to see which features are used and improve the app: for example, that an identification was made (with the edibility category it gave and the confidence), that a catch was published or deleted, that an ad was shown or that Pro was bought (with the amount). In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland it is only enabled if you accept it in Google's form; in the rest of the countries it is enabled by default and, if the form appears, it follows your answer. We do not send your name, location, photos or the text of your catches, and the app does not use the iPhone advertising identifier for this.

What is kept on your device

Your account session, your settings (language, map layers, map view, last tab) and whether you have Pro are kept on the device itself, so the app comes back the way you left it. The fishing places (from OpenStreetMap) and the tide stations are built into the app.

Your rights

You can ask for access, correction or deletion by writing to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com, although you can already do almost everything from the app itself.

Notice: Important: the AI can be wrong, and tides are an astronomical prediction without wind or waves. Do not rely on FishSpot alone to go out to sea or fish from rocks, and always check your local fishing rules: minimum sizes, closed seasons, bag limits and licenses change by place and year.
