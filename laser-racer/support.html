Laser Racer

Support and privacy

In short: the game never asks for or stores any personal data. There is no account, no sign-up, no email and no password. What leaves your device is: the ads (Google AdMob), the purchase check (RevenueCat), minimal usage analytics on iPhone and iPad (Google Analytics for Firebase) and, only if you play online with someone else, the networked race and the leaderboards (Supabase). Analytics and personalised ads are switched on only with your consent where the law requires it.

What the game is

Laser Racer is a fast spaceship game. You fly through canyons and landscapes on two planets, dodging obstacles, shooting and holding out with your shield. There are ten worlds —five on each planet— and five races in each world.

Game modes:

  • Campaign: the races of all ten worlds, one after another.
  • With friends: two to four pilots, on the same device or online, joining with a room code or being matched with strangers.
  • Survival: last as long as you can. It unlocks once you finish the whole Campaign, and it plays offline.

There are four ships —Halcón, Aguja, Mamut and Cometa—, each handling differently. You can play with the touchscreen, a keyboard or a controller. The game is in Spanish and English.

What is free and what costs money

The download is free and shows ads. The free version is a demo: one world to play on your own, one world to play with someone else, and one ship, the Halcón.

There are two purchases, both one-off payments (not subscriptions: they do not renew and nothing else is charged):

  • Remove the banner (€1.99 in Spain; your country's exact price is shown by the store): removes the banner from the menus. The full-screen ads stay. It is the only purchase.

Individual ships may be sold later on. If that changes, this page is updated.

What is stored, and where

Nothing personal. No name, no email, no account. The game does not read your contacts, your location, your photos, your camera or your microphone.

Your progress —races completed, worlds and ships unlocked, upgrades, language, volume and controls— is stored on your device only. It is not uploaded anywhere and not synced, and it is deleted when you uninstall the game.

Playing online

Online "With friends" and the leaderboards use Supabase, a database and realtime service the developer pays for. It works like this:

  • Your device opens an anonymous session: a random identifier created when you install the game. It carries no email, no name, no account and not the device's advertising identifier.
  • Your pilot name is not typed by you: the game makes it up from a fixed word list plus three digits (for example "Solar Halcón 417"). There is no text field anywhere in the game.
  • During a race only each player's controls and the room data are sent: chosen ship, "ready", the made-up name and the game version. No video, no voice, and nothing written by you.
  • There is no chat, neither text nor voice. There is no way to send a message to another player.
  • Leaderboards store the made-up name, the score, the mode, the duration and the game version.
  • As with any internet connection, the server receives your IP address. It is the developer's own server: it is not sold or handed to anyone.

If you play alone, or with someone on the same device, no connection is needed and none of this is sent.

Ads and consent

The game shows Google AdMob ads:

    the one-off €1.99 purchase
  • a full-screen ad before a race starts, with an on-screen warning first, and at most one every 90 seconds. Free version only;
  • an app open ad when you open the game, or come back to it after thirty minutes or more away, on one in every five openings;
  • an optional "Keep playing" rewarded ad: only if you choose to watch it, after losing, to carry on where you were. It never appears on its own.

The first time you open the game, and before any ad is requested, this happens, in this order:

  1. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form.
  2. On iPhone and iPad, a short Google screen explaining tracking and then Apple's prompt asking whether you allow the app to track you.
  3. Only then does AdMob start, and only if the consent step allows it.

If you allow it, AdMob may use the device's advertising identifier to personalise and measure ads; if you do not, ads still appear, but unpersonalised or limited to what your choice allows. Either way Google receives technical device data such as the IP address. Saying no is perfectly fine: the game works exactly the same.

Changing your mind: in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the "Ad privacy" button in the game's Settings reopens Google's form; from then on ads follow your new choice. The tracking permission is changed in iPhone or iPad Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. The game's Settings also link to this page.

Usage analytics

Laser Racer uses Google Analytics for Firebase on iPhone and iPad to know how many people play and when, so the game can be improved.

  • What is collected: automatic open, session and screen-view events; an app-installation identifier on your device, which is not your name, your email or the advertising identifier; and basic technical data: approximate country, operating system, app version and device model.
  • What is not collected: the advertising identifier (IDFA) is not collected with Analytics, no user ID or personal properties are set, and nothing from your progress is sent. Analytics does not trigger Apple's tracking prompt.
  • What for: usage statistics only. They are not linked to your name, your email or any account —Laser Racer has no accounts— and are not used to follow you across other companies' apps or websites, or for advertising.
  • With whom: Google processes them as a data processor. The installation identifier is shared with RevenueCat so that purchases appear in the same statistics.

Consent and control. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland analytics stays off until you answer Google's consent form on opening the game: it is only switched on if you accept storing data on your device, and if you refuse, no usage data is collected. The "Ad privacy" button in Settings reopens that form. Outside that area, where prior consent is not required, analytics is on by default; you can ask for it to be stopped by writing to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com.

Legal basis: usage analytics, with your consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland (which you can withdraw at any time) and, outside that area, legitimate interest in improving the game. Networked play, because it is necessary to provide the service you ask for by joining a room.
