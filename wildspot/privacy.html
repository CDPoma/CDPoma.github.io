Privacy policy

WildSpot · Help & support

Summary: WildSpot identifies fruit trees, trees, shrubs, plants, flowers, cacti, lizards, bugs and bees from a photo with AI, and saves them as finds with their place on a map. It is a private social network: only your friends, whom you add with a code, see what you save; there is no public feed and nothing is anonymous. For each find you decide who sees it (only you or your friends) and, separately, whether your friends see where it is; you can close it later. The account is just a display name: no email or password. To identify, the photo is sent to our server and from there to Claude, by Anthropic; it is not kept unless you save the find. The app shows Google AdMob ads and the Pro subscription removes them. Usage statistics are anonymous. Deleting your account deletes everything.

Who is responsible

Gabriel Micó Victoria, developer of WildSpot. For any question about your data, or to exercise your rights, write to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com.

Account

There is no email, phone number or password. The map opens without a name. To identify, see the feed, save finds, have friends, like and comment you need a display name (2 to 30 characters) and to accept the community guidelines: choosing it creates your account, an anonymous Supabase account that is given that name. We recommend a nickname rather than your full name.

Your name is seen only by you, your friends and anyone who has a friend request with you. Likes and comments are seen by the owner of the find and the owner's friends: for likes, the names of your friends are shown and "and N more" for the rest; on comments, someone who is not a friend of the author sees "Friend of…" instead of their name. Your account session is stored encrypted in the iOS Keychain of this device, and does not travel in backups or to another iPhone: with a new iPhone you start with a new account. Your account and data are stored on Supabase, on a server in the European Union (Frankfurt).

Who sees your finds and where they are

When you save a find we store the photo (a copy of up to 1,600 pixels on the longer side, re-encoded as JPEG, which removes its metadata, such as the photo's GPS), the common and scientific name (the AI's or the one you correct), the category, the AI's confidence, what the AI said about danger, fruit or advice, your optional note (up to 500 characters) and the date. You control two things separately, and you can change them later:

  • Who sees it? "Only me" or "My friends". With "Only me", nobody else sees anything. There is no public option: nobody who is not your friend can see your finds or their photos, not even knowing their address.
  • Do my friends see where it is? A separate switch. Off, your friends see the photo, name, note, likes and comments, but neither the coordinates nor the point on the map nor any hint of the place. You always see your own. If the AI thinks it may be a protected species, the place starts hidden and you decide whether to show it. With a photo from your gallery the switch starts off.

When you close the location or stop sharing, it stops being visible at once; but what a friend has already seen may have been copied, and the app warns you. Photos are stored in private Supabase storage and the app shows them with signed links that expire in 5 minutes; they can only be signed if the person asking can see the find. A link a friend already had signed may keep working until it expires (5 minutes at most). A photo that never becomes a saved find is deleted automatically after a day.

Friends, likes and comments

Each account has a 6-character friend code. Anyone with your code can send you a request, and you are friends only if you accept it; you can change your code if someone you do not want has it. Your friends see your name and the finds you show them. Only people who see a find can like and comment on it (a comment has up to 280 characters). You can delete your comments, and the owner of a find can delete any comment on it. If you remove a friend or block them, what was visible between the two of you is cut at once; blocking also deletes the friendship and requests, and you cannot become friends again without a new request.

Identifying a plant or a bug

When you take or choose a photo, the app makes a reduced copy of it (about 1,024 pixels on the longer side) and re-encodes it as JPEG, which removes its metadata. That copy is sent to our server, with the app language, and from there to Anthropic's artificial intelligence service (Claude), which returns the name, the category, the confidence, what it looked at, what it could be confused with and, where relevant, danger warnings, fruiting months and a respect tip. The photo is not saved to your photo library.

  • We do not keep the photo or the result unless you save the find; the photo being identified is not uploaded to storage until you save it.
  • We do not give Anthropic your account, your name or your position. Our server does not log the photo or the AI's text.
  • Identifying needs a display name. We count how many identifications each account makes per day (the limit is 40 per account per day) and, to stop abuse, there is also a daily cap per IP address and one for the whole app; the IP is stored only as a hashed fingerprint (HMAC), never in plain form. Those counters do not include the photo or the result.
  • If the photo is of a person, nobody is identified. If it is a mushroom, a bird or a fish, the app says so and you can save it under "Other".
  • The identification can be wrong: it is not a guarantee that something is edible or harmless, and it does not replace a field guide or an expert.

Location and map

WildSpot asks for location permission while you use the app, not in the background: to put you on the map, guide you to a find with "Go" and, if you want, save where you found it. Your exact position reaches our server only as the place of a find you save with a location (or the point you choose by hand on the map). The compass, bearing and distance are calculated on your device. To draw your finds and your friends', the app sends our server the rectangle of the map you are looking at and the categories switched on; the server returns only what you are allowed to see, and we do not store that rectangle. If you do not give permission, you can mark the place by hand; you can also save a find without a place, if you confirm it. "Directions by car" opens Apple Maps with the destination. The map and satellite imagery are Apple's (MapKit).

Checking for a new version

On iPhone and iPad, when the app starts it asks Apple's App Store (itunes.apple.com) which is the latest version and, if there is one with important changes, tells you and takes you to its page. Apple receives the app identifier and your IP address. If the check fails, the app keeps working.

Reporting and blocking

Anyone can report a find or a comment (for dangerous information, offensive content, spam or another reason) and block its author. What you report stops being visible to you at once; whatever collects 3 reports from different people is hidden from everyone except its author and kept for the developer to review, within 24 hours. In addition, a word filter rejects offensive comments, notes and names.

Deleting your data

  • One find: delete it from its page. It is removed together with its photo, its place, its likes and its comments.
  • One comment: deleted by its author or by the owner of the find.
  • Your whole account: Feed → your avatar → ⚙ → Delete my account. Your name, finds with their photos and places, comments and likes, friendships and requests, reports and blocks and usage counters are deleted. It cannot be undone. (Photos are deleted from storage at once or, at the latest, by the sweep that runs every 15 minutes.)

Ads and purchases

The free version shows Google AdMob ads: a banner, a full-screen ad while each photo is being identified and, from time to time, one when the app opens. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent before showing ads; then iOS asks whether you allow tracking (App Tracking Transparency). You can change your answer whenever you like, and the "Ad privacy options" item in Settings lets you reopen the form where Google requires it. If you do not allow tracking, you still see ads, but not personalised ones. Google may receive data from your device and the ads according to its privacy policy.

Pro is a monthly or yearly subscription managed by Apple's App Store, with RevenueCat to verify your purchase: RevenueCat receives the anonymous identifier of your account and your purchases, not your name or your location. With Pro no ads are shown.

Usage statistics

We use Firebase Analytics, anonymously and without the advertising identifier, to see which features are used and improve the app: for example, that an identification was made (and its category), that a find was saved (and whether it had a place), that a like or a comment was made, that a friend was added, that an ad was seen or that Pro was bought, and server errors. Your name, your coordinates, the name of what you find and the text of your notes are never sent. They are switched on only if Google's form allows it (in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, if you consent), and they are never used for advertising.

What is kept on your device

Your account session (in the iOS Keychain), your settings (language, tab, map categories and layers, 3D view, feed filters, your last choice of "Who sees it?" and "My friends see where it is") and whether you have Pro are kept on the device itself, so the app comes back the way you left it.

Your rights

You can ask for access, correction or deletion of your data by writing to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com, although you can already do almost everything from the app itself.
