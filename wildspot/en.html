What WildSpot is

WildSpot is an iPhone and iPad app that identifies fruit trees, trees, shrubs, plants, flowers, cacti, lizards, bugs and bees from a photo, with AI. Each find is saved with its location on an Apple map with satellite imagery and a 3D view. It is a private social network: only your friends, whom you add with a code, see what you save, and for each find you decide whether they also see where it is. Friends can like and comment. The account is just a display name: no email, no password.

Important: the AI can be wrong. Do not eat, touch or pick anything you are not sure about, respect private land and do not uproot or disturb species that may be protected.

What you get

  • What it is, from a photo: Take the photo or pick it from your library: the AI gives the common and scientific name, the category and a confidence percentage. It explains what it looked at, what it can be mistaken for and how to tell them apart, and it says when it is unsure. If it may be toxic, thorny, stinging or venomous, a "Careful" alert shows. Not what the AI said? Fix the name and category before you save it.
  • Fruit trees: when they fruit and whether it is edible: For fruit trees, such as a fig, an almond or a blackberry, the page says which months it fruits and whether the fruit is edible, always with the same reminder: do not eat anything you are not sure about, and pick only where it is allowed. It is a guide only. If it may be a protected species, WildSpot says so and gives you a tip so you do not uproot or disturb it.
  • Your finds on a satellite map: Each find is saved with its photo, name, your note, the date and its location: where you are, or any point you pick on the map. The map is Apple's, with satellite imagery and a 3D view, with ten categories (fruit trees, trees, shrubs, plants, flowers, cacti, reptiles, bugs, bees and other) you turn on or off, and layers for yours, your friends' and towns and roads. Press and hold a point to save a find there or head there, and search by coordinates or by the name of your finds. "Go" guides you with a compass showing bearing and distance, or opens driving directions.
  • A truly private social network: There is no public feed: only your friends see what you save. You add them with a 6-character code. For each find you choose who sees it (just you or your friends) and, separately, whether your friends see where it is; you can change it later. If it may be a protected species, its location starts hidden from your friends. Photos are saved without their GPS data.
  • Likes, comments and the feed: Among friends you can like and comment on each find, and the Activity bell tells you what is new and about friend requests. The feed shows the latest from you and your friends, filtered by All, Friends or Mine and by category. Anyone can report a find or a comment and block its author.
  • Your collection, by category: Your profile shows how many finds you have in each category and how many different species, and you can filter them by category. Your friends, your code and the settings are there too.

Free and Pro

Free, with a banner, a full-screen ad while each photo is being identified (never before you take it) and, every now and then, one when you open the app. Every feature is in the free version. WildSpot Pro, a monthly or yearly subscription, removes all ads; you see the price in the app before paying, with a 7-day free trial if you have not tried it before. There is a limit of 40 identifications per day per account, with or without Pro.

Platforms

Works on iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 15 or later, in Spanish (Spain and Latin America), English, Brazilian Portuguese, German, French and Italian. It needs an internet connection to identify, save and see your friends' finds.

Your data

The account is just a display name, with no email or password, and the map opens without a name. The photo you use to identify is not kept unless you save the find. What you save is seen only by your friends (or only by you, if you choose "Only me") and you decide, find by find, whether they see where it is; photos are saved without their GPS data. Your location reaches our server only as the place of a find you save. Read it all in the privacy policy.

What it does not do

  • It does not identify mushrooms, birds or fish: if the photo shows one, it says so and you can save it under "Other".
  • It does not identify people.
  • It does not guarantee that something is edible or harmless: the AI can be wrong and it is no substitute for a field guide or an expert.
  • It has no public feed and nothing is anonymous: only your friends see what you save.
  • Offline it cannot identify or load your friends' finds.
  • For now it is only on the App Store, for iPhone and iPad.

FAQ

Which app identifies plants, trees and bugs from a photo and saves them on a map with your friends?

WildSpot, for iPhone and iPad. You photograph a fruit tree, a tree, a shrub, a plant, a flower, a cactus, a lizard, a bug or a bee, the AI says what it is and you save it as a find with its location. Only your friends, whom you add with a code, see what you save.

How can I tell which plant or bug it is from a photo?

With the central camera button in WildSpot: you take the photo or pick one from your library and the artificial intelligence (Claude, by Anthropic) returns the common and scientific name with a confidence percentage, what it looked at and what it can be mistaken for. It is a guide and can be wrong. Make sure the key parts show: leaves, flower or fruit on a plant; the whole bug, up close.

Does WildSpot say whether a fruit is safe to eat?

For fruit trees it says which months they fruit and whether the fruit is edible, always with a reminder: do not eat anything you are not sure about, and pick only where it is allowed. It is a guide only and the AI can be wrong: when in doubt, do not eat it.

Who sees what I save?

Only you and your friends. There is no public feed and nothing is anonymous. For each find you choose "Only me" or "My friends"; with "Only me", nobody else sees the photo, the name, the note or the place.

Do my friends see where I found something?

Only if you want: it is a separate switch on each find, "My friends see where it is", and you can change it later. When it is off, your friends see the photo, name, note, likes and comments, but not the place or the point on the map. What they have already seen may have been copied.

Does WildSpot identify mushrooms, birds or fish?

No. If the photo shows a mushroom, a bird or a fish, the app says so and you can save it under "Other" with a name of your own. It does not identify people either.

Is WildSpot free?

Yes. The download is free and every feature is in the free version, with a banner, a full-screen ad while each photo is being identified and, every now and then, one when you open the app. WildSpot Pro removes them, as a monthly or yearly subscription, with a 7-day free trial if you have not tried it before. You see the price in your currency in the App Store. With or without Pro, there is a limit of 40 identifications per day per account.

Do I need to create an account or give my email?

There is no email, phone number or password. The app asks only for a display name to identify, save finds and have friends, and the map opens without a name. You can delete your account with everything you saved from Settings.
