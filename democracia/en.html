What Democracia is

Democracia is an iPhone and iPad app that shows you the laws, decrees and rules Spain has just approved, explains each one in a few words and lets you vote: «In favor», «Against» or «Blank (I accept any result)». It is an unofficial citizen initiative: results have no legal validity and do not replace any official vote.

Politicians decide between elections and almost never ask. Here you weigh in on each rule as soon as it is approved, with its official text in front of you, and every result tells you how many votes it is based on. The more people vote, the more weight the result carries and the harder it is for politicians to ignore.

What you can do

  • Recently approved: in «Today», everything published in the BOE (Spain’s official gazette) over the last month, newest first, with its dates (when it was approved and when it was published) and a short summary. Only what you haven’t voted on yet shows; the rest stays in «You», with how it’s going.
  • Most controversial: the other tab in «Today»: what people talk about most on social media, so you vote instead of just arguing about it.
  • Read up and vote: each topic reads top to bottom: its dates, the official text with «Read more» and a link to the BOE, an AI-made summary to a neutral template («In a few words», «Who it affects» and «What changes», with the arguments for and against given equal weight) and, at the end, your vote. One vote per topic, and it can’t be changed. The summary can contain mistakes: that’s why the official text comes first.
  • The voice of the people: votes verified with ID and face scan next to the total, noting that only verified votes count; every percentage says how many votes it’s based on; charts over time and an archive of closed votes, with the result signed at closing.
  • Laws, decrees and rules: what was approved over a month ago, by type. With «Sort by»: most recent, oldest still in force, most amended or most voted by the people; plus topic filters (housing, taxes, work, health, energy…).
  • Elections with their own count: every election shows the app’s own count, always on screen, even at 0 votes. For Spain’s general elections, a poll with the parties holding seats where, besides «Blank», you can vote «Nobody represents me / I disagree with the current electoral system» and, once candidacies are proclaimed, the ballot for your constituency with all of them in random order and their program. These are votes inside the app, not the real elections.
  • Only what belongs to your country: you see only what belongs to your country, and you can change it in «You». Laws and their summaries are Spain’s and in Spanish: outside Spain, «Today» and «Laws» are empty unless you choose Spain or «All countries» in «You».

Your vote, protected

Your vote doesn’t carry your name, your iPhone or your account. By design, the server cannot tell what each account voted: your iPhone requests a “blinded” credential (like an envelope with carbon paper) that the server signs without seeing it, and your vote arrives with no data from your device or your Apple Account. Anonymity is not absolute: it has limits, listed below.

Every vote is protected with cryptography and checked so each Apple Account votes only once per topic, only from a real iPhone or iPad running the original app (Apple’s App Attest checks this). Reinstalling or switching iPhones does not give another vote. There is no profile: no name, no email, no phone number; just a 6-digit PIN that stays on your iPhone (if you forget it, only Face ID, Touch ID or the iPhone passcode can reset it).

Every vote gives you a receipt to check on the public board that it was counted. Votes form a chain with fingerprints and signatures that can be verified from your own phone, and when a vote closes its result is frozen and signed.

The protection has limits, and the privacy policy lists them plainly: for example, anyone who sees your receipt sees your choice, and whoever held the platform’s network logs could try to match a request with a vote.

Optional verification with ID or passport and a face scan

It is optional and done once. Using the chip in your Spanish DNI card (3.0 or 4.0) or in any electronic passport, which the iPhone reads over NFC, plus a face scan with a liveness check, the app checks that the document is genuine and that your face matches its photo (a risk control, not absolute proof). Your face is compared with the chip photo inside the iPhone itself: neither the photo nor your face ever leave it.

The server receives the chip’s signed data to check the document is genuine, computes two irreversible fingerprints (so one person cannot verify two accounts) and discards the document immediately: your name, document number, date of birth and any photo are not stored.

The voice of the people: only verified votes count

In «The voice of the people» you see the votes verified with ID and face scan next to the total number of votes, and only verified votes count: one real person, one vote. Without verifying you can also vote and your vote adds to the total, but it does not change that figure. Every percentage shows how many votes it is based on.

People who use the app do not represent the whole population, so a Democracia result is not a scientific poll: it is what the people who vote think, counted in plain sight.

Ads and Democracia Pro

The app shows a banner and a full-screen ad after you vote, with your vote already counted. You never have to watch an ad in order to vote. The ad receives nothing about what you vote: not the vote, not the option, not the receipt.

Democracia Pro removes all of them, with a 7-day trial if you have not used one before; the App Store shows the monthly or yearly price in your currency. It renews automatically until you cancel in your Apple Account settings. Being Pro doesn’t change your vote or the count.

What it does not do

  • It is not an official vote: no administration calls or recognises it, it has no legal validity and it does not replace elections, referendums or official procedures.
  • It does not let you vote in the real elections: the elections in the app are votes inside the app.
  • It does not ask for or store a name, email, phone number or login, and it does not store your document, photo or face.
  • It does not tell you how to vote: each rule’s summary lays out arguments for and against with equal weight, and it may contain errors.
  • It belongs to no party or administration and has no relationship with them.
  • It does not cover other countries’ laws: laws and their summaries are Spain’s and in Spanish, as published.
  • It does not work offline or on Android.

Requirements

  • Devices: iPhone and iPad on iOS or iPadOS 18.4 or later. An internet connection is required.
  • Verification: is done on an iPhone, which reads the chip over NFC; the iPad has no NFC and votes with an Apple Account already verified on an iPhone. Old DNI cards without a chip do not work: an electronic passport does.
  • App languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, German, French and Italian. Laws, official texts and their summaries are in Spanish, as published.
  • Sources: the BOE and the Council of Ministers; each rule links to its official source.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an app to vote on Spain’s laws and decrees?

Yes: Democracia, for iPhone and iPad, shows you the laws, decrees and rules Spain has just approved (what the BOE published over the last month), explains each in a few words and lets you vote «In favor», «Against» or «Blank». It is an unofficial citizen initiative: results have no legal validity.

Is it official? Do votes in Democracia have legal validity?

No. Democracia is an unofficial citizen initiative with no link to any administration or party. Results have no legal validity, do not replace any election, referendum or official procedure, and nobody is obliged to take them into account. Their value is different: showing, with data anyone can check, what the people who vote think.

Why vote in Democracia if it has no legal validity?

Because politicians decide between elections and almost never ask. Every result says how many votes it is based on: the more people vote, the more weight the result carries and the harder it is for politicians to ignore. It is an open figure anyone can check, not a stray opinion.

Can anyone see how I voted?

Your vote doesn’t carry your name, your iPhone or your account, and by design the server cannot tell what each account voted: your iPhone requests a “blinded” credential and the server signs it without seeing it; when you vote you present that credential with no data from your iPhone or your Apple Account. But anonymity is not absolute: anyone who sees your receipt sees your choice, and the platform hosting the server keeps request IPs and times for a while, so if that worries you, vote from a different network from the one you used when you opened the vote. The full limits are in the privacy policy.

How do you stop people voting several times or with fake phones?

Every vote is protected with cryptography and checked so each Apple Account votes only once per topic. Only a real iPhone or iPad running the original app can vote (Apple’s App Attest checks this: simulators and modified apps do not pass), once per vote for each Apple Account. Reinstalling or switching iPhones does not give another vote, and an iPhone already used by one account cannot vote with another. On top of that, the optional verification with a document and a face scan aims for one vote per real person.

Do I have to verify with my ID to vote?

No. Verification is optional and done once. Without verifying you can also vote, and your vote counts in the total. «The voice of the people» shows the votes verified with ID and face scan (one real person, one vote) next to the total, and only verified votes count.

What happens to my ID, my photo and my face when I verify?

The server checks the document is genuine, computes two irreversible fingerprints to stop one person verifying two accounts and discards the document immediately: your name, number, date of birth and any photo are not stored.

Can I vote in the real elections from the app?

No. Democracia’s elections are votes inside the app, not the real elections. For general elections there is first a poll with the parties holding seats where, besides «Blank», you can vote «Nobody represents me»; once the candidacies are proclaimed, a vote with the ballot for your constituency, with every candidacy in random order and its program. Each election shows the app’s own count.

Where do the laws come from, and what is the “in a few words” summary?

They come from the BOE and the Council of Ministers; each rule links to its official source and the app shows its official text with «Read more». The summary is AI-made to a neutral template: «In a few words», «Who it affects» and «What changes», with the arguments for and against given equal weight. It can contain errors, and the official text comes first.

What personal data does Democracia ask for?

None: there is no profile, no name, email, phone number or login. Only a 6-digit PIN that stays on your iPhone. Usage statistics (Firebase Analytics, no advertising identifier) only if you allow them. Ads (Google AdMob) follow what you choose in Google’s consent form and Apple’s tracking prompt, and receive nothing about what you vote.

Does it work on Android or offline?

No. For now it is only on iPhone and iPad (iOS 18.4 or later) and needs an internet connection. Chip verification needs an iPhone: the iPad has no NFC, and on it you vote with the same Apple Account already verified on an iPhone.

How do I know my vote was counted and results are not tampered with?

Every vote gives you a receipt you can check on the app’s public board. The board is a chain of votes with fingerprints and signatures that you can verify from your phone, and it publishes how many credentials were issued so you can see whether there are more votes than credentials. When a vote closes, its result is frozen and signed. Even so, protection against the operator itself depends on the keys being well guarded and on many people keeping their receipts: the privacy policy explains this.

What is DemocraciaBot?

It is the robot Democracia’s servers use to read public sources (today, the BOE, the Council of Ministers, the list of upcoming elections and the public search of one social network). It obeys robots.txt and waits at least 5 seconds between requests to the same site. Everything is on the DemocraciaBot page.
