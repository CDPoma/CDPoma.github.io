Privacy policy

Níscalo · Help & support

Summary: Níscalo identifies mushrooms from a photo using artificial intelligence, lets you save your places (spots) on a 3D satellite map and publish your finds. To identify, save spots or publish you need an account, which has no email or password: it is only a display name you choose. The map and the wall can be viewed without an account. Your spots are private until you decide to share them with your friends or on the wall. For each find you choose who sees where you found it (only you, your friends or everyone), but the photo, the description and the probable species of a published find are public to anyone, even without an account. If you switch on the optional mushroom alert, we store an approximate area of about 10 km and your device's notification token, linked to your account. The app shows Google AdMob ads, and the Pro subscription removes them. Usage statistics are anonymous.

Who is responsible

Gabriel Micó Victoria, developer of Níscalo. For any question about your data, or to exercise your rights, write to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com.

Account

To identify, save spots, publish, add friends, report or block, you need an account. The account has no email and no password: it is an anonymous Supabase session with only a display name that you choose and that other people see on your finds, on the spots you share and in your friend requests. We recommend a nickname rather than your full name. If you delete the app, you start from scratch, and you can delete your account in Settings → Delete my account. Your account is stored in Supabase, with servers in the European Union (Frankfurt).

Identifying a mushroom

When you tap "Identify", the app reduces the photo and sends it to our server, and from there to Anthropic's artificial intelligence service (Claude). It is only used to give you the identification.

  • We do not keep the photo or the result unless you publish the find.
  • We count how many identifications each account makes per day (the limit is 40 per account per day), to apply the usage limit. That counter does not include the photo.

Location and compass

Níscalo asks for location permission only when it needs it: when you save a spot with GPS, when you publish a find with a location, when you center the map on your position and on the "Go" screen, which uses your position and the compass to lead you to a place. Your position only reaches our server when you save a spot or publish a find with a location and, if you switch on the optional mushroom alert, an approximate area of about 10 km (see "Mushroom alerts" below). The compass, the bearing and the distance are calculated on your device. If you do not grant permission, you can mark the place by hand on the map.

Your spots

A spot stores its name, an optional note, its coordinates and whether you marked it with GPS or by hand on the map, linked to your account on our server. A spot is private: only you see it, until you decide otherwise:

  • With a friend: the friends you choose see it, with your display name. You can stop sharing it, and the person who received it can also remove it from their list.
  • On your wall: anyone sees it, even without an account, with your display name. You can take it off the wall whenever you like.
  • By WhatsApp or another app: Níscalo opens the system share menu with the name, the coordinates and an Apple Maps link. What happens next depends on the app you choose and on whoever receives it.

You can delete a spot from its page. When you delete your account, all your spots are deleted.

Friends

Each account has a 6-character friend code that only you see. Anyone with your code can send you a friend request, and you are only friends if you accept it. Your friends see your display name, the spots you share with them and the location of the finds for which you choose "friends". You can remove a friend or block them: doing so stops what had been shared between the two of you.

Publishing a find

When you publish, we store the photo, the description (optional), the probable species, the confidence and the edibility guide returned by the identification. All of this is public: anyone can see it, also without an account, on the wall and on the map, with your display name.

The location is separate. If you grant permission, the GPS position of the find is stored and you choose who sees it: only you (the default), your friends or everyone. The place is stored even if you choose "only you", so that you can see it; nobody else can. After publishing, that choice cannot be changed: to change it, delete the find and publish it again.

  • Without location permission, the app warns you and you can publish without a location, if you confirm.
  • Do not publish photos of people or data you do not want everyone to see. If you leave the location visible to everyone, it may reveal where you forage.

The map and the terrain

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, the large maps (the Map tab and the "Go" screen) use Apple's native map (MapKit), with its satellite imagery in 3D, and the small maps (a spot's page and choosing a point) use Apple's map for the web (MapKit JS). Apple receives the map requests, the area you are looking at and your IP address. We do not give Apple any account or identifier of ours, and Níscalo does not send it your spots or your finds: the app places the pins on the map on your device.

On top of the map there are five layers you switch on or off. "Towns and roads" are the names on Apple's own map. "Trails and tracks" and "Streams and springs" use OpenStreetMap data served by OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org). "Contour lines" are computed on your device from the elevation tiles of AWS Terrain Tiles / Mapzen (Amazon S3, based on NASA SRTM data), and the elevation, slope and sun or shade readout uses that same elevation data. "Pine forests" (on by default) shades in orange the conifer woodland areas of the Copernicus Land Monitoring Service (2018), served by the European Environment Agency (image.discomap.eea.europa.eu); it only requests tiles inside Europe and when the map is zoomed in, and it uses no personal data. Each of these servers receives your IP address and the area of the map you look at (the tiles requested), like any web server; never your account or your name.

On Android and on the web, the map uses Esri satellite imagery instead (World Imagery, server ibasemaps-api.arcgis.com, with an API key) and the same OpenFreeMap and AWS layers. Esri also receives your IP address and the area you look at, never your account or your name. The Android version is not in the store yet.

So that Apple loads the map for the web (MapKit JS), the app asks our server (Supabase) for a temporary, short-lived permit. That request carries no personal data: not your name, not your location and not your spots.

If you tap "Directions" (on a spot, a find or the card of a map point that you open by touching and holding) or "Directions by car", the app opens your maps app with the coordinates of the place; from there, that app's privacy policy applies.

Mushroom alerts (optional)

On iPhone and iPad you can switch on the "Saffron milk caps may be out tomorrow" alert in Settings. It is off by default, and Níscalo asks for notification permission only when you switch it on.

To send it to you we store on our server (Supabase), linked to your account, your device's notification token, your language, your time zone and an approximate area of about 10 km: that of your most recent spot or, if you have none, your last known position. Your exact position never reaches the server for this alert.

Once a day our server asks Apple WeatherKit for the weather of that area, with no account or personal data, and decides whether to send the alert, at most one every 3 days. Delivery goes through Apple's Push Notification service (APNs), which receives your device's token.

If you switch the alert off, we stop checking the weather for your area and stop sending notifications, and your device's record becomes inactive. If you delete your account, that record is deleted entirely. We do not use this data for anything else.

Checking for a new version

On iPhone and iPad, when the app starts it asks Apple's App Store whether a newer version exists and, if an update is required, tells you. For that, Apple receives the app identifier and your IP address, like any web server.

Reporting and blocking

You can report any public find or spot and block its author. Blocking hides their posts from you and ends the friendship and anything shared between the two of you. A post or a spot that collects 3 reports is hidden automatically and kept for review by the developer.

Deleting your data

  • One find: delete it from its page. It is removed together with its photo and its location.
  • One spot: delete it from its page.
  • Your whole account: Settings → Delete my account. Your profile, finds with their photos and locations, spots, friendships and requests, reports, blocks, usage counter and your device's alert record are deleted. The sign-in account is deleted too, unless you use it in another of our apps.

Ads and purchases

The app shows Google AdMob ads (a banner and a rewarded video you watch before the photo is analysed). In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google asks for your consent before showing ads; you can change it in the same form. On iPhone and iPad, Apple asks whether you allow tracking (ATT); if you do not, ads are not personalised with that tracking.

Pro is a subscription managed by Apple's App Store, with RevenueCat to verify your purchase: RevenueCat receives the anonymous identifier of your account, not your name. Pro removes the banner and the video. Apple handles the payment; we only learn whether the subscription is active.

Usage statistics

We use Firebase Analytics anonymously to see which screens are used and improve the app. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland it is only enabled if you accept it in Google's form; in the rest of the countries it is enabled by default and, if the form appears, it follows your answer. We do not send your email, name, location or the text of your finds, and the app does not use the iPhone advertising identifier.

Your rights

You can ask for access, correction or deletion by writing to gabrielmicovictoria@gmail.com, although you can already do almost everything from the app itself.

Safety warning: Níscalo does not replace the opinion of an expert mycologist. No photo-based identification is infallible. Do not eat any mushroom if you have the slightest doubt, and always check with an expert before consuming any specimen.
