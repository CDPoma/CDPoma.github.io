What Níscalo is

Níscalo is an app for iPhone, iPad and Macs with Apple silicon that identifies mushrooms from a photo using artificial intelligence and saves your places on a terrain map. It does not replace an expert mycologist.

Important: Níscalo does not replace an expert mycologist. AI identification is a guide only and can be wrong. Never eat a mushroom just because an app says so: if you have the slightest doubt, don't eat it, and ask an expert or a local mycological society.

What you get

  • One photo, a likely species: Take a photo with the camera or pick one from your library and see the common and scientific name of the most likely species, with a confidence percentage. When confidence is low, the app tells you not to rely on it.
  • Know what it can be confused with: Each result shows the features visible in the photo and the lookalike species, including dangerous ones, plus an edibility guide (edible, eat with caution, not edible, toxic, deadly or unknown), always shown with the safety warning.
  • A map made for the woods: Three layers: Satellite, with Apple's aerial imagery; Mountain, terrain only, with hillshading, elevation colors and labeled contour lines; and Streets.
  • Elevation, slope and sun or shade: On the Mountain layer, the point under the crosshair shows elevation, slope and whether the slope faces the sun or lies in shade, using NASA SRTM elevation data.
  • Your spots stay yours: Save a place with GPS or by tapping the map. It stays private until you share it with friends, post it to the wall or send it in a message with another app. The "Go" screen gives you the map and a compass with bearing and distance.
  • Finds and friends, on your terms: Post what you find with a photo, a description and the location, and choose who sees the place: only you, your friends or everyone. Add friends with a code. No email and no password: just a name.

Free and Pro

Free to download, with a banner and a short full-screen video before each photo analysis. Níscalo Pro removes both, billed monthly or yearly, with a 7-day free trial if you haven't tried it before; the price in your currency is shown in the App Store before you subscribe. Up to 40 photo analyses per day per account.

Platforms

Works on iPhone, iPad and Mac computers with Apple silicon. An internet connection is required. Mac has no compass, so the "Go" screen shows bearing and distance.

Your data

Your account is just a name, with no email or password. Your spots are private until you share them, and for each find you choose who sees the place. The photo you identify with is not kept unless you publish the find. The privacy policy explains everything.

What it does not do

  • It does not tell you whether you can eat a mushroom: it gives a clue, not a green light, and it can be wrong.
  • It does not replace an expert mycologist or a local mycological society.
  • It does not identify without an internet connection.
  • It does not publish your spots: they are private until you share them with friends or on the wall.
  • It does not show where you found something to anyone but the people you choose: only you, your friends or everyone.

Frequently asked questions

Does Níscalo tell me whether I can eat a mushroom?

No. Níscalo gives a clue, not a green light: it suggests the most likely species with its confidence and an edibility guide, and it can be wrong. It does not replace an expert mycologist. Never eat a mushroom just because an app says so; if you have the slightest doubt, don't eat it, and ask an expert or a local mycological society.

How does Níscalo identify a mushroom?

From a photo you take with the camera or pick from your library. The AI returns the common and scientific name of the most likely species, a confidence percentage, the features visible in the photo and the species it can be confused with, including dangerous ones. Photograph the cap, gills and stem for a better result. There is a limit of 40 analyses per day per account, and an internet connection is required.

Which map does it use and what does the Mountain layer show?

The map is Apple's, with three layers: Satellite, Mountain and Streets. Mountain shows terrain only: hillshading, elevation colors and labeled contour lines. Point the crosshair and it shows elevation, slope and whether the spot faces the sun or lies in shade, using NASA SRTM elevation data.

Are my spots private?

Yes. A spot is yours alone until you share it with friends, post it to the wall or send it in a message with another app. For finds, you choose who sees where you found them: only you, your friends or everyone. Your account is just a name, with no email or password.

Is it free, and which devices does it run on?

It is free to download and runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac computers with Apple silicon. The free version shows a banner and a short video before each photo analysis. Níscalo Pro removes them, with a 7-day free trial if you haven't tried it before; the price in your currency is shown in the App Store before you subscribe. Mac has no compass, and if your Mac has no camera you identify from a photo in your library.
